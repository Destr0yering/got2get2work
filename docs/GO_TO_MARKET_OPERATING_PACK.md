## 1. Galaxy listing copy

### App name

Got2Get2Work

### Tagline options

- Coordinate smarter commutes
- Shared rides for the same shift
- A simpler way to get to work

### Short description

Coordinate rides with people headed to the same workplace at the same time.

### Long description

Got2Get2Work helps people coordinate potential shared rides with others headed to the same destination at compatible times.

The app is designed for recurring commutes such as shifts, classes, training programs, and other repeat destinations. Users can set their schedule, share a general pickup area instead of a required home address, review compatible options, and decide together before any ride is confirmed.

Got2Get2Work is built around worker control and privacy:

- review schedule-based matches before taking action;
- use approximate locations before revealing a public meeting point;
- confirm or decline every ride request yourself;
- handle changes with recovery options when a plan falls through;
- manage blocking, feedback, and account controls in the app.

Got2Get2Work coordinates possible carpools. It does not provide transportation, employ drivers, guarantee a ride, or certify that another participant is safe to ride with.

This Galaxy release is a closed beta focused on:

- profile setup;
- schedule confirmation;
- matching and ride requests;
- cancellation and recovery workflows;
- product feedback from early testers.

If you want to join the beta or share feedback, contact `beta@got2get2work.com`.

## 2. Partner pitch

### One-line pitch

Got2Get2Work helps workers headed to the same site at the same time coordinate practical shared rides without turning commute data into employee surveillance.

### Short pitch

Got2Get2Work is a worker-directed commute coordination product for fixed-shift teams, campuses, and recurring destination communities. It helps participants discover compatible ride options based on destination and timing overlap, while partners receive only aggregate pilot reporting. The product is designed to improve commute reliability without requiring home-address collection, route surveillance, or guaranteed transportation claims.

### 20-minute partner call structure

#### Opening

We are preparing a small pilot for organizations that see recurring commute friction around one site or shift cohort. Got2Get2Work helps participants coordinate shared rides while keeping the individual experience voluntary, privacy-conscious, and user-controlled.

#### Problem frame

- off-peak or variable-hour commutes are hard to cover;
- some participants absorb repeated transportation cost or attendance risk;
- organizations often see the pattern but lack a low-friction, privacy-safe coordination layer.

#### What the product does

- organizes workers by destination and compatible timing;
- supports schedule review and recurring coordination;
- uses approximate areas before revealing more specific meetup details;
- requires mutual acceptance before a ride is confirmed;
- supports recovery when a ride or shift plan changes.

#### What partners receive

- aggregate enrollment and activation counts;
- aggregate match, ride, and recovery activity;
- pilot observations about adoption friction and commute patterns;
- no home locations, personal messages, exact routes, or trip histories in the standard product.

#### Best-fit pilot

- one worksite or campus;
- one recurring destination pattern or difficult shift cohort;
- 10 to 25 opted-in participants to start;
- 21 to 60 day evaluation period;
- one internal owner for participant communication and feedback.

#### Call to action

If the use case fits, the next step is a pilot-design conversation to define the launch group, communication plan, success metrics, and privacy boundary.

### Objection handling

#### Is this transportation?

No. Got2Get2Work coordinates potential carpools between participants. It does not operate vehicles, employ drivers, or guarantee rides.

#### Do managers see employee commute details?

No. The standard partner view is aggregate-only. Individual routes, messages, and exact saved locations are not part of routine partner reporting.

#### What if a ride falls through?

The product is designed to support recovery by surfacing next-step options and helping the participant re-coordinate. Recovery is part of the beta evaluation because commute plans do change.

#### Why start with one site?

A single-site pilot reduces variables, improves density, and makes the evidence more trustworthy before expanding.

## 4. Beta-test package

### Beta purpose

This beta is meant to validate whether people can:

- complete setup without live assistance;
- confirm a schedule the app can use;
- understand compatible match options;
- request or accept a ride confidently;
- recover when a plan changes;
- report privacy, safety, or usability concerns.

### Tester criteria

- Samsung Galaxy device user;
- adult participant;
- recurring destination such as work, school, training, or a regular site;
- willing to share a general commute area and honest product feedback;
- understands the app coordinates potential rides and does not guarantee transportation.

### Tester welcome copy

Thanks for helping test Got2Get2Work. This beta is about whether the product is understandable, useful, and respectful of privacy during real commute planning. We are especially testing schedule setup, matching, mutual coordination, and recovery when a plan changes.

Please use your real impressions. If something is confusing, slow, or feels off, that is valuable feedback.

### Tester task script

1. Install the beta build and open the app.
2. Create an account and review the consent screens.
3. Set or confirm your schedule for a real recurring destination.
4. Provide a general pickup area rather than a precise home address.
5. Review any compatible ride options shown.
6. If available, send or respond to a ride request.
7. Review the coordination flow and any meeting-point detail shown after mutual acceptance.
8. Trigger or simulate a change if the test flow allows it, then review the recovery path.
9. Find the safety, block, feedback, export, or deletion controls.
10. Report what felt easy, unclear, or risky.

### Feedback prompts

- What step felt most intuitive?
- Where did you hesitate or feel unsure?
- Did the privacy boundary feel clear?
- Would you use this for a real commute if enough coworkers or peers were present?
- What almost stopped you from continuing?
- If a commute plan changed, did the recovery flow make sense?
